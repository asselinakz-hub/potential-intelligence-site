The problem

Sales teams coach the behavior they can see — not the architecture that creates it.

A rep misses follow-up. Another avoids urgency. A third builds trust easily but struggles to close. The usual answer is more training. Often it does not explain why the pattern repeats.

"Same product. Same training. Same CRM. Different outcomes."
The pilot starts from this question: what is different in the rep's natural sales architecture?
1
Performance variance is not always a skill gap.
Sometimes the issue is role fit, energy cost, decision style, or how a seller naturally creates trust and urgency.
2
Generic coaching misses the real friction point.
One rep needs structure. Another needs urgency activation. Another needs emotional safety. Same coaching does not unlock everyone.
3
Role fit is often judged too late.
A person may struggle in transactional outbound but thrive in enterprise discovery or expansion. This framework helps see that pattern earlier.
Concrete scenario

Two AEs. Same onboarding. Same quota. Different results.

What the framework reveals
The difference is not effort or skills — it is behavioral architecture.
Marcus may be naturally strong in strategic discovery and trust-building (high vectors) but has a high-effort relationship with operational follow-through and urgency activation. Knowing this changes the coaching conversation completely. Instead of "work harder on closing" — it becomes "let's build a follow-up structure that removes the energy cost."

FAQ

Common questions

Is this a personality test?
No. The report maps revenue behavior patterns — how a person reads buyers, creates value, moves deals, and responds to different sales environments. It is designed for coaching and role design, not personality labeling.
Is this a performance evaluation?
No. This is a validation-stage research report. It should not be used to judge, rank, hire, fire, or penalize anyone. The goal is learning — for the participant, the manager, and the methodology.
What if a participant disagrees with their report?
That is exactly what we need to know. Disagreement is the most valuable feedback in a pilot. It helps us improve the questions, scoring logic, and interpretation before the product launches. No report should be shared without a follow-up conversation.
Can a manager see the report?
Only with the participant's agreement. The pilot works best when the report becomes a shared coaching tool — not a judgment delivered from above.
Are the scores scientifically validated?
Not yet — this is an early-stage pilot. The scoring logic is being tested through participant and manager feedback. That is the explicit purpose of this validation stage.
How long does the assessment take?
Approximately 10–14 minutes. The questions focus on real sales behavior patterns — not hypothetical preferences or trait descriptions.
