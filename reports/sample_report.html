Top coaching priorities

What the manager should work on first

01
Stalled-deal protocol

Deals do not die at close — they die when silence starts. Ask: “Show me your three most silent deals. What did you last do, and when?” Build a 48-hour protocol for each.

02
Reframing → commitment bridge

This rep’s reframing is their superpower: buyers shift, clarity arrives. But momentum stops there. Teach the bridge: “Based on this, what decision needs to happen before we talk again?”

03
Cost-of-delay language

Their natural urgency mechanism runs through cost of inaction, not emotional pressure. Ask: “What gets worse for the buyer in 30 days if nothing changes? Can you quantify that?”

Manager view

From symptom to action

What the manager sees

Good discovery. Engaged buyers. Positive conversations. But deals slow down after the buyer says “this is interesting.”

What the report reveals

The issue may not be effort or product knowledge. The pattern is clarity without commitment.

What to try next

Coach the seller to convert buyer clarity into mutual commitment before the next customer conversation ends.

One sentence for the next 1:1

“Your strength is helping the buyer see the problem differently. Your next level is converting that clarity into a committed next step before the deal loses energy.”

Important boundary: This is a coaching hypothesis based on self-reported input and should be validated through real conversations, manager observation, and deal behavior. It is not a standalone performance evaluation.
