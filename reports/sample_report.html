Where this seller’s execution may be slowing the deal — and what the manager should try next.

Example seller: Enterprise Account Executive · Complex B2B sales · Strong discovery, inconsistent deal movement
One-line manager read: This seller creates trust and buyer clarity, but may lose momentum when a good conversation needs to become a committed next step.
Why this matters

Experience tells you what the seller has done before. This report shows how that experience may show up now.

Two sellers can have the same background, same training, same CRM, and same manager — but very different execution patterns. Potential Intelligence looks at the layer between knowledge and results: how this person tends to move a buyer, build trust, respond to pressure, and convert interest into action.

Manager view

From symptom to manager action

What the manager sees

Good discovery. Engaged buyers. Positive conversations. But deals keep slowing down after interest is created.

What the report reveals

The issue may not be effort or product knowledge. The pattern is clarity without commitment: the seller creates understanding but does not always secure the next decision step.

What to try next

Coach the seller to convert buyer clarity into mutual commitment before the next customer conversation ends.

Manager action plan

What to coach in the next 1:1

The goal is not to tell the seller to “be more proactive.” The goal is to change one specific behavior before the next customer conversation.

1
Inspect one stalled opportunity.

Choose a deal where the buyer was engaged but the next step became vague or delayed.

2
Find the missing commitment moment.

Ask where the seller created clarity but did not ask for a decision path, stakeholder commitment, or calendar-specific next step.

3
Test one behavior next week.

Have the seller end the next customer call with a clear decision question, not a generic follow-up.

Suggested 1:1 sentence

“Your strength is helping the buyer understand the problem. Your next level is converting that clarity into a committed next step before the deal loses energy.”

Important boundary: This is a coaching hypothesis and decision-support report. It should be validated through real conversations and observed deal behavior.
